About This Session

Build the tool you keep wishing you had.

You know that game, quiz, or center activity you always wanted but could never find (or afford)? Now you can just make it. You describe what you want in plain words, the AI writes the code, and you spend your time trying it out and telling it what to fix.

In three hands-on hours you will go from an idea to a working web activity your students can open on any device. It lives in tools you already have, like Google Sheets and free GitHub Pages, so there is no subscription and no student data leaving your school. You will build something real, learn where to host it, and pick up a few simple habits for keeping it safe.

🧑‍🏫

Who it's for

Elementary and middle school teachers, plus coaches and librarians who work with them. No tech background required.

🎒

What to bring

A laptop, a Google account, and a free AI chatbot login (ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini). A free plan is enough to follow along.

🎁

What you'll leave with

A live link to an activity you built during the session and a simple plan for using it with students next week.

Agenda

Four build blocks, start to finish.

We open and close with a quick ticket, and every block ends with a short activity to keep the room moving from idea to working tool.

Entry Ticket

0–10 minReimers

On our WonderWall question wall, drop one classroom task you wish had a better tool (a warm-up, a review game, a way to collect answers). Your ideas become the things we build together today.

Block 1 · What Is Vibe-Coding?

10–50 minGuhlin

See how it works: you describe the tool, the AI writes it, you test and fix. We walk the friendly VIBES steps (Vision, Instruct, Build, Evaluate, Ship) and practice the most important one, the Vision sentence: "This is for ___ so they can ___." Watch a tool get built live from a plain-language prompt.

Quick move: Write your own Vision sentence and share it with a neighbor.

Block 2 · Build Your First Tool

50–105 minReimers · Guhlin floating

Hands on the keyboard. Pick a starter using Peggy's ready-to-play activities as models (a grouping game, an idiom picker, a match-up, or a digital breakout), then build your own version from a copy-and-paste prompt. Connect it to your own Google Sheet so answers save automatically. Add one thing at a time so you can see what changes. Goal: a working tool before the block ends.

Quick move: Gallery walk. Post your live link on the shared board and try two classmates' tools.

Block 3 · Get It to Your Students

105–140 minReimers

Now put it where kids can reach it: free GitHub Pages, a Google Sites page, an email link, or your learning platform. We cover the one hosting trap that trips people up and the easy fix. Then the student-safety basics: make names optional, skip email, use a simple code instead, and check your district's rules first.

Quick move: Vote on the hosting option that fits your school and name one thing that might get in the way.

Block 4 · Keep It Safe

140–170 minGuhlin

A friendly look at doing this responsibly. Ask the one question that sorts every tool fast: does this touch student data? Walk a short safety checklist you can run with the AI itself, then the three questions to answer before any tool goes live, including who keeps it running if you move classrooms.

Quick move: Ask the AI to review your tool for safety and note one thing it flags.

Exit Ticket

170–180 minGuhlin

Back on WonderWall, post the tool you built, where you put it, and the next one you want to make. You leave with a live link in hand.
