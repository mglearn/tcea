## Phases of Learning (John Hattie, Visible Learning)

| Phase    | What the student is doing                       | SOLO levels                     |
|----------|-------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------|
| Surface  | Building facts, vocabulary, basic skills        | Unistructural, Multistructural  |
| Deep     | Connecting ideas, explaining how they fit       | Relational                      |
| Transfer | Applying learning to a new, unfamiliar situation| Extended Abstract               |

Key principle: "What and when are equally important." A strategy that works in one
phase can fail in another. Match the instructional move to the phase the student is in.

## SOLO Taxonomy (Structure of Observed Learning Outcome)

- Pre-structural: no meaningful engagement; misses the point.
- Unistructural (Surface): one relevant idea, no connections.
- Multistructural (Surface): several ideas as a list, not connected.
- Relational (Deep): ideas connect into a coherent whole; explains relationships.
- Extended Abstract (Transfer): generalizes to new situations; creates, hypothesizes.

Watch for the understanding-vs-production gap: a student may reason at a high level
aloud but produce low-level written work (or vice versa). Diagnose the barrier
(vocabulary, mechanics, language proficiency) before concluding a phase.

## ACE Framework (next steps, built on SOLO)

- Articulate It: student names/states ideas. Use when a student is at Surface and
  needs to solidify and express foundational knowledge.
- Connect It: student links ideas together. Use when moving Surface -> Deep.
- Extend It: student applies to new contexts. Use when moving Deep -> Transfer.

## High-Effect-Size Instructional Strategies (effect size >= 0.40 accelerates learning)

| Strategy                    | Effect size | Best phase       |
|-----------------------------|-------------|------------------|
| Jigsaw Method               | 0.92        | Surface + Deep   |
| Argumentation               | 0.86        | Deep             |
| Outlining & Organizing      | 0.85        | Deep             |
| Critical Thinking           | 0.79        | Deep + Transfer  |
| Transfer Strategies         | 0.75        | Transfer         |
| Reciprocal Teaching         | 0.74        | Deep             |
| Concept Mapping             | 0.66        | Deep             |
| Vocabulary Programs         | 0.62        | Surface          |
| Problem-Solving Teaching    | 0.61        | Transfer         |

Timing rule: Problem-Solving Teaching and other Transfer strategies only work AFTER
Surface and Deep foundations are in place. Used too early, they slow progress.

## How to score a phase from item data

1. Group items by the phase each item measures (the scoring key defines this).
2. A student who succeeds only on Surface items is at Surface.
3. A student who succeeds on Surface + Deep (connecting/explaining) items is at Deep.
4. A student who also succeeds on the transfer/application item is at Transfer.
5. When evidence is mixed, name the highest phase the student CONSISTENTLY shows,
   and note the barrier blocking the next phase.

## Known Gen AI failure modes to avoid

- Generic output that ignores the actual student data.
- Activities suggested before pre-assessment/readiness is established.
- Invented effect sizes, fake studies, made-up citations.
- Surface-only design that never moves students to Deep or Transfer.
