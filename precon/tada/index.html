How it works

Three steps, three files. No special tool, no subscription.

1

Score a scenario

Pick a scenario below. Read the story, the five questions, and five real student answers. Decide 1 (correct) or 0 (not yet) for each.

2

Enter scores & export to CSV

Open the spreadsheet, go to that scenario's tab, type your 1s and 0s, and export the tab to CSV.

3

Get a report from any chatbot

Upload the CSV with the chatbot-pack files. The chatbot returns each student's phase of learning and specific next steps — plus follow-up prompts you can use.

Privacy: students are listed as S01, S02… Never put real student names into a public AI tool.

Session agenda

Three hours: learn the frameworks, apply them by hand to a scenario, then validate your judgment with Gen AI.

·

Entry Ticket · 5 min · 0:00–0:05

Quick poll: "How confident are you turning assessment data into next-day instruction?" Sets the baseline.

1

Intro to the Frameworks · 40 min · 0:05–0:45

Phases of learning (surface, deep, transfer), the SOLO Taxonomy, the ACE Framework, and high-effect-size strategies matched to phases. Opening Jigsaw: small groups each take one phase/strategy pairing and teach it back.

2

Apply It by Hand · 50 min · 0:45–1:35

Score a scenario: read the story, questions, and student answers, and decide each student's phase. Plan interventions and differentiation for each.

·

Break · 10 min · 1:35–1:45

3

Validate with Gen AI · 55 min · 1:45–2:40

Set up a chatbot for success, export your scores as a CSV, run the analysis, and compare the AI's report against your by-hand judgment — where they agree, where they differ, and which to trust.

4

From Analysis to Teaching · 15 min · 2:40–2:55

Share out about the data and process; modify teaching to match each student's phase; debrief across groups.

·

Exit Ticket · 5 min · 2:55–3:00

"What's the first thing you'll try, and what's one question you still have?"

01Reading: Story RetellingK-2 · 3 surface · 1 deep · 1 transfer▼ open
The context: The class read The Little Red Hen. The Little Red Hen finds some wheat and asks her friends the cat, the dog, and the pig to help her plant it, harvest it, and bake it into bread. Each time, they say "Not I!" So she does all the work herself. When the bread is ready, everyone wants to eat it, but the Little Red Hen says no and eats it with her chicks.
Questions & what a correct answer looks like
surfaceQ1. Who are the characters in the story?
Correct means: Names the Little Red Hen and at least one other animal (cat, dog, pig).
surfaceQ2. What did the Little Red Hen do first, next, and last?
Correct means: Gives events in order: plant/find wheat, then harvest, then bake.
surfaceQ3. Where does the story take place?
Correct means: Identifies a farm or the Hen's home.
deepQ4. Why did the Little Red Hen not share the bread?
Correct means: Explains that the friends refused to help, so they didn't earn it.
transferQ5. If you were the Little Red Hen, what would you do, and why?
Correct means: Gives a choice AND a reason connected to fairness or helping.

03Science: Water Cycle3-5 · 3 surface · 1 deep · 1 transfer▼ open
The context: After a week studying the water cycle, Mr. Lee gave a short entry ticket with a blank water-cycle diagram and a few questions. Students have seen the terms evaporation, condensation, and precipitation and watched a demonstration with a covered bowl of warm water.
Questions & what a correct answer looks like
surfaceQ1. Label the three stages on the diagram (arrows point to the sun/water, the cloud, and the rain).
Correct means: Labels evaporation, condensation, precipitation in the right places.
surfaceQ2. Match the term to its meaning: evaporation / condensation / precipitation.
Correct means: Matches all three terms correctly.
surfaceQ3. Which stage is happening when a cloud forms?
Correct means: Answers condensation.
deepQ4. Explain how water gets from the ocean up into a cloud.
Correct means: Connects evaporation (heat turns water to vapor) rising to condensation (vapor cools into a cloud).
transferQ5. Why does it rain more in a rainforest than in a desert? Use the water cycle in your answer.
Correct means: Applies the cycle to explain more evaporation/moisture in a rainforest vs. a desert.

04Reading: Main Idea & Inference3-5 · 2 surface · 2 deep · 1 transfer▼ open
The context: Students read a one-page passage about a girl named Priya who spends her Saturday building a bird feeder after noticing fewer birds in her yard. The passage never states why she does it; readers must infer. Vocabulary word in context: "scarce."
Questions & what a correct answer looks like
surfaceQ1. What did Priya build on Saturday?
Correct means: Answers a bird feeder.
surfaceQ2. The passage says birds had become "scarce." What does scarce mean here?
Correct means: Defines scarce as few or hard to find.
deepQ3. What is the main idea of the passage?
Correct means: States that Priya acted to help the birds she noticed were disappearing.
deepQ4. Why did Priya build the feeder? (The passage doesn't say directly.)
Correct means: Infers she wanted to bring birds back or help them find food.
transferQ5. Think of a problem in your own neighborhood. What could you build or do to help, like Priya did?
Correct means: Applies Priya's problem-solving to a new, plausible situation with a reason.

06Science: Ecosystems & Energy Flow6-8 · 3 surface · 1 deep · 1 transfer▼ open
The context: A middle-school science class studied food chains and energy flow in a grassland ecosystem: grass, grasshoppers, mice, hawks. This entry ticket checks understanding before a unit on human impact.
Questions & what a correct answer looks like
surfaceQ1. In a food chain, what is a producer?
Correct means: Defines producer as an organism that makes its own food (e.g. grass/plants).
surfaceQ2. In grass -> grasshopper -> mouse -> hawk, which is the top consumer?
Correct means: Answers the hawk.
surfaceQ3. Put these in order from producer to top consumer: mouse, grass, hawk, grasshopper.
Correct means: Orders grass, grasshopper, mouse, hawk.
deepQ4. Why is there less energy available to the hawk than to the grasshopper?
Correct means: Explains energy is lost at each level, so less reaches the top.
transferQ5. If a disease killed most of the mice, what would happen to the hawks and the grasshoppers? Explain.
Correct means: Predicts hawks decline (less food) and grasshoppers rise (fewer predators), with reasoning.

09Biology: Cellular Respiration9-12 · 3 surface · 1 deep · 1 transfer▼ open
The context: A high-school biology class studied cellular respiration: glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, and the electron transport chain. This entry ticket checks understanding before a lab on fermentation.
Questions & what a correct answer looks like
surfaceQ1. What is ATP and what is its role in the cell?
Correct means: Identifies ATP as the cell's energy currency/molecule.
surfaceQ2. What are the reactants and products of cellular respiration?
Correct means: Reactants: glucose + oxygen; products: carbon dioxide + water + ATP.
surfaceQ3. Where in the cell does the Krebs cycle occur?
Correct means: Answers the mitochondria (matrix).
deepQ4. Explain how glycolysis connects to the Krebs cycle.
Correct means: Explains glycolysis produces pyruvate, which feeds into the Krebs cycle.
transferQ5. A cell is deprived of oxygen. Predict what happens to its ATP production and why.
Correct means: Predicts ATP drops / shifts to fermentation because the electron transport chain needs oxygen.

S02Energy.Glucose and oxygen.Mitochondria.They're connected.The cell dies.
S03ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is the cell's main energy currency, releasing energy when a phosphate is removed.Reactants: glucose + O2. 10ELA: Rhetorical Analysis9-12 · 2 surface · 2 deep · 1 transfer▼ open
The context: Students read a famous short speech excerpt and analyzed how the speaker persuades. This entry ticket checks whether they can identify and evaluate rhetorical choices before writing an analysis essay.
Questions & what a correct answer looks like
surfaceQ1. Identify one appeal in the excerpt: ethos, pathos, or logos.
Correct means: Correctly names an appeal present in the text.
surfaceQ2. What is the tone of the excerpt?
Correct means: Names an appropriate tone (e.g. urgent, hopeful).
deepQ3. Analyze one rhetorical choice the speaker makes and its purpose.
Correct means: Identifies a specific device and explains its intended effect.
deepQ4. How effective is that choice for the speaker's audience? Explain.
Correct means: Evaluates effectiveness with reasoning tied to the audience.
transferQ5. Choose a different audience (e.g. young children). How would you rewrite the appeal to fit them?
Correct means: Adapts the rhetorical strategy appropriately to a new audience.
Sample student answers to score
StudentQ1Q2Q3Q4Q5
S01Pathos, it appeals to emotion.Urgent and determined.The speaker repeats a phrase to build emotional momentum and make the idea memorable.It's effective because the audience was fighting for a cause and the repetition unites them and stirs feeling.For young children I'd use a simple repeated line and a picture, like 'We can help. We can share.' to keep it emotional but easy.
S02Pathos.Serious.He uses big words.It works.I would make it shorter.
S03The speaker uses logos by citing the promise of founding documents as evidence.Resolute and hopeful.The extended metaphor of a 'promissory note' frames justice as a debt owed, making an abstract idea concrete and morally urgent.Highly effective for an audience seeking justice: it reframes their demand as collecting what they are legally owed, not asking a favor, which dignifies the cause.For young children I'd swap the metaphor for a broken promise about a treat: 'Someone promised everyone a turn, and it's not fair if some kids never get one' to keep the fairness logic in concrete terms.
S04Ethos.(blank)The speaker talks to the crowd.(blank)Make it for kids.
S05Pathos and repetition.Passionate.The repetition of a key line emphasizes the main hope and makes it stick.It's mostly effective because it's memorable, though some listeners might want more specifics.For younger kids I'd use a short chant they can repeat so they feel included, keeping the emotional appeal.
