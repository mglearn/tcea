① Surface · Build the knowledge

Phase 1 — Build the knowledge

Before students can solve the town square problem, they need the raw material: the words, the facts about how communities work, and how to read a map. These three activities are fast and front-loaded — the goal is acquisition, not yet analysis.

🎯 By the end of Phase 1 students can use the key words, tell why people form communities, name ways communities meet their needs, and read a simple map with its parts.
Vocabulary & feedback · d 0.62§113.14(c)(2)(A)

1 · Word bank & vocabulary sort

Teach the unit words. Then let students sort them twice: first into "needs" vs. "wants," then into their own groups (give feedback on their reasoning). Keep the words on the wall — the whole unit uses them.

WordKid-friendly meaning
communitya group of people who live, work, and help each other in one place

wantsomething that is nice to have but you can live without

Jigsaw method · d 0.92§113.14(c)(2)(B), (c)(14)(A)

2 · Jigsaw reading — How do communities meet their needs?

Split the class into four expert groups, each studying one topic below, then re-mix into home groups where every topic is represented. Each expert teaches their group. (Jigsaw is one of the highest-leverage surface moves because every student must teach.)

Direct instruction · d 0.56§113.14(c)(4)(C), (c)(3)(B)

3 · Map of the town

Show students a simple map of a town. Point out and name the five map elements: title, compass rose, legend, scale, and grid. Then have students find and mark community services on the map — the school, the fire station, the park, the market, and the town square. Talk about how people change the land to build these (§(c)(3)(B)).

Quick write: "On my map, the ______ is at grid ______. To get there from the school, I go ______ (use the compass rose)."
