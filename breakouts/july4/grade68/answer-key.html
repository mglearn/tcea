📜 The Draft in the Statehouse

statehouse-student.html
  1. Lock 1 — Central Claim [mc]
    According to Source A, what does the Declaration hold to be "self-evident"?
    Answer: "That all men are created equal, with unalienable rights" (option 2)
    Why: Source A states the self-evident truth: all men are created equal, endowed with unalienable rights including Life, Liberty, and the pursuit of Happiness. That is the opening principle, quoted directly.
  2. Lock 2 — Source of Power [word]
    Source B says just government powers come from the "consent of the ______." Type the missing word.
    Answer: governed
    Why: Source B states power is derived from "the consent of the governed." The missing word is governed — the idea that legitimate authority comes from the people.
  3. Lock 3 — Date Lock [digit]
    Per Source E, in what YEAR was the Declaration adopted? (Four digits.)
    Answer: 1776
    Why: Source E gives the adoption date as July 4, 1776. The year is 1776 — the event Independence Day commemorates.
  4. Lock 4 — Argument Structure [seq]
    Using Sources A, C, and D, put the three parts of the Declaration's argument in the order they appear.
    Answer: principle → grievances → conclusion
    Why: The Declaration is structured as principle (all are equal, with rights) → evidence (the list of grievances) → conclusion (therefore, independence). Recognizing that argument structure is the point.

🔎 Case File: Cause & Effect

  1. Lock 1 — Root Cause [mc]
    Which slogan captured the colonists' main objection to the new taxes?
    Answer: ""No taxation without representation"" (option 2)
    Why: The taxes evidence states the core objection: colonists were taxed by a Parliament where they had no representatives — "no taxation without representation."
  2. Lock 2 — The Protest [word]
    In 1773, colonists dumped British tea into which harbor? Type the city name.
    Answer: boston
    Why: The evidence says the Boston Tea Party took place in Boston Harbor in 1773 — a protest against the tea tax and monopoly.
  3. Lock 3 — Order of Events [seq]
    Put these four events in the order they actually happened.
    Answer: taxes → tea → acts → dec
    Why: The chain runs: new taxes (from 1765) → Boston Tea Party (1773) → Britain's harsh laws in response (1774) → Declaration of Independence (1776). Order matters for cause and effect.
  4. Lock 4 — Causes vs. Not-Causes [multi]
    Select EVERY item the evidence supports as a real cause of colonial anger. Reject anything that just happened nearby or after.
    Answer: Select: #1, #2 (reject the rest)
    Why: The real causes named in the evidence are the taxes-without-representation and Britain's harsh response. The warm summer is a coincidence, and the Declaration was an EFFECT of the buildup — not a cause of it. "Before" or "at the same time" does not mean "caused."

🕊️ The Unfinished Promise

promise-student.html
  1. Lock 1 — Ideal vs. Reality [mc]
    Based on the evidence, in 1776 did the reality match the written ideal that "all men are created equal"?
    Answer: "No — slavery was legal and many were denied rights" (option 2)
    Why: The reality evidence is explicit: slavery was legal, women could not vote, and many were excluded. The ideal was stated, but the reality did not match it in 1776.
  2. Lock 2 — Abolition Year [digit]
    In what YEAR did the 13th Amendment abolish slavery? (Four digits.)
    Answer: 1865
    Why: The evidence dates the 13th Amendment to 1865, after the Civil War — the legal end of slavery in the United States.
  3. Lock 3 — Extending the Vote [word]
    The 19th Amendment (1920) extended the right to vote to which group? Type one word.
    Answer: women
    Why: The evidence says the 19th Amendment barred denying the vote on account of sex — extending the vote to women nationwide.
  4. Lock 4 — What Extended the Promise [multi]
    Select EVERY item the evidence shows actually extended rights toward the ideal. Reject anything that only sounds patriotic.
    Answer: Select: #1, #2, #3 (reject the rest)
    Why: The three amendments/laws are documented steps that extended rights. The song is inspiring but changes no law, and the claim that everyone was equal in 1776 is contradicted by the evidence. Follow the record, not the rhetoric.
