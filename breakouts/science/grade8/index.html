▶ Featured breakout

Launch Control

Premise: A rocket is in its final countdown. Students act as the launch director and must clear three systems — the fuel chemistry (conservation of mass), the thrust math (a = F ÷ m), and the telemetry link (waves) — by solving four locks before the launch window closes.

Students open the evidence board and solve four locks (a mass-conservation sequence, a Newton's-Second-Law multiple-choice, a mass-conservation evidence sort, and a wave word lock). Each lock reveals a short reasoning explanation when solved. The answer key is password-protected, not shown on this page.

🎯 Why these concepts

Every lock targets a documented Grade 8 misconception — the ideas that most depress achievement on state science assessments:

🚨 Misconceptions this breakout confronts: «mass disappears when fuel burns» (atoms only rearrange — in a sealed system mass is conserved); «a heavier rocket accelerates faster because it's bigger» (for fixed force, more mass means less acceleration); and «amplitude and wavelength are the same thing» (amplitude is energy; wavelength is crest-to-crest distance).

🔧 STEM extension — Engineering Design Challenge

Pair the breakout with a hands-on build that makes Lock 1 real. Following TEA's engineering design process (define → imagine → plan → create → test → improve), teams run a reaction in a sealed bag (baking soda + vinegar), massing it before and after to prove conservation of mass — then run a straw-rocket extension that varies payload mass to test a = F ÷ m.

Anchored in engineering-practice TEKS

  • 8.1(B) use engineering practices to design solutions to problems
  • 8.1(G) develop and use models to represent solutions
  • 8.2(B) analyze data / sources of error; 8.2(D) evaluate designs
  • 8.6(E) / 8.7(A) (anchor content) conservation of mass & Newton's Second Law
