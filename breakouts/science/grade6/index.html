▶ Featured breakout

Cargo Ship Rescue

Premise: The freighter TEKS Voyager is riding too low, a deck crate keeps sliding, and the loading crane keeps losing power. Students act as the harbor engineer, read six pieces of evidence, and solve four locks — using density, net force, Newton's Third Law, and energy conservation — to clear the ship to sail.

Students open the evidence board and solve four locks (an energy-order sequence, a density multiple-choice, a balanced-forces evidence sort, and a Newton's-Law word lock). Each lock reveals a short reasoning explanation when solved. The answer key is not shown on this page.

🎯 Why these concepts

Every lock targets a documented Grade 6 misconception — the ideas that most depress achievement on state science assessments:

🚨 Misconceptions this breakout confronts: «heavy things always sink» (density vs. volume); «a moving object must have an unbalanced force on it» (net force = 0 can still mean stillness or steady motion); «Newton's Third-Law pairs cancel each other out» (they act on different objects); and «energy gets used up» (it transfers and transforms — it is conserved).

🔧 STEM extension — Engineering Design Challenge

Pair the breakout with a hands-on build so students apply the density concept from Lock 2. Following TEA's engineering design process (define → imagine → plan → create → test → improve), teams design an aluminum-foil cargo barge that carries the most pennies before sinking, then redesign to beat their first score.

Anchored in engineering-practice TEKS

  • 6.1(G) develop and use models to represent solutions to engineering problems
  • 6.2(D) evaluate experimental and engineering designs
