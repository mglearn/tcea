▶ Featured breakout

Rescue Run

Premise: A hiker is missing in a Big Bend canyon and a storm is closing in. Students act as the search-and-rescue command team: they read a drone's distance–time graph, tell speed from velocity, and reason about how heat keeps the medical cooler warming — then solve four locks to launch the rescue.

Students open the evidence board and solve four locks (a graph-story sequence, a speed-vs-velocity multiple-choice, a graph-reading evidence sort, and a heat-transfer word lock). Each lock reveals a short reasoning explanation when solved. The answer key is not shown on this page.

🎯 Why these concepts

Every lock targets a documented Grade 7 misconception — the ideas that most depress achievement on state science assessments:

🚨 Misconceptions this breakout confronts: «a flat line on a distance–time graph means moving at a steady speed» (it means stopped); «speed and velocity are the same» (velocity adds direction and depends on displacement); «a downhill line means slowing down» (it means returning); and «insulation adds cold / stops heat» (it only slows heat, which always flows hot→cold to equilibrium).

🔧 STEM extension — Engineering Design Challenge

Pair the breakout with a hands-on build that turns Lock 4 into a real test. Following TEA's engineering design process (define → imagine → plan → create → test → improve), teams design an insulated container to keep an ice cube frozen longest — then graph temperature vs. time and read the slope, exactly like the drone's motion graph.

Anchored in engineering-practice TEKS

  • 7.1(B) use engineering practices to design solutions to problems
  • 7.1(G) develop and use models to represent solutions
  • 7.2(B) analyze data for patterns / sources of error; 7.2(D) evaluate designs
  • 7.8(A–B) (anchor content) heat transfer & thermal equilibrium
