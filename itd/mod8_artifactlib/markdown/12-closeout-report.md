# Project Closeout Report: Help Desk Workflow Redesign

**Project window:** August 17 - December 18, 2026  
**Sponsor:** Chief Technology Officer  
**Project lead:** Help Desk Manager  
**Closeout status:** Accepted with two follow-up actions

## Original Problem

Teachers and campus staff reported slow first response, unclear ticket status, repeated handoffs, and inconsistent escalation. Baseline analysis showed 3,200 tickets per quarter, median first response of 18.4 business hours, and 61% first-contact resolution.

## Original Project Goals

1. Reduce median first response to 4 business hours or less for normal-priority tickets.
2. Establish one districtwide priority and escalation model.
3. Increase first-contact resolution to at least 70%.
4. Give users clear ticket status without exposing internal technical notes.
5. Provide principals with a simple campus service dashboard.

## Scope Delivered

| Deliverable | Status | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Priority definitions and SLA targets | Complete | Approved service guide |
| Queue and assignment redesign | Complete | Updated workflow and roles |
| User-visible status updates | Complete | Platform configuration |

## Stakeholder Feedback

**Teachers:** Most positive comments cite faster acknowledgement and clearer status. Remaining concerns focus on long resolution times for vendor-dependent issues.

**Principals:** Weekly campus report is useful when it highlights aged tickets rather than only total counts.

**Technicians:** Clear ownership reduced "ticket ping-pong," but the team requested better internal documentation for recurring classroom display issues.

## What Went Well

- The project defined service measures before changing the tool.
- Technician involvement in workflow design improved adoption.
- A single escalation rule reduced handoff ambiguity.
- Leadership dashboard emphasized user experience, not simply number of tickets closed.

## What We Would Change Next Time

- Collect two full months of baseline data before setting all targets.
- Involve two classroom teachers in the initial workflow workshop rather than only in user testing.
- Pilot automated notifications with one campus before districtwide release.
- Separate vendor-wait time from internal IT resolution time earlier in the project.
