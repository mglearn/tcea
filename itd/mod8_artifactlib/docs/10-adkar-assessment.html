Change Readiness Assessment: Staff MFA Rollout

Framework: ADKAR - Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement
Purpose: Diagnose where adoption is likely to break down and match support to the actual barrier.

Rating Scale

  • 3 - Ready: Evidence suggests the group can move forward.
  • 2 - Watch: Some gaps exist; targeted support is needed.
  • 1 - At risk: Significant barrier could prevent adoption.

Group Assessment

ADKAR Diagnostic Notes

Awareness - Do people understand why the change is necessary?

Evidence: Staff survey shows 86% understand that account compromise is a district risk, but only 62% can explain why a password alone is insufficient.

Action: Keep the explanation short and concrete: MFA reduces the value of a stolen password. Avoid turning staff communication into a cybersecurity lecture.

Desire - Do people see enough value to participate?

Evidence: Teacher focus group concerns center on inconvenience and personal-phone expectations rather than disagreement with security goals.

Action: Explain alternate-factor options, show the actual login experience, and use campus leaders to reinforce that enrollment is an operational expectation with support available.

Knowledge - Do people know what to do?

Evidence: Pilot users complete enrollment successfully when they follow the quick guide, but recovery questions are common.

Action: Add a 90-second enrollment video, one-page guide, FAQ, and help desk recovery script.

Ability - Can people perform the new behavior in real conditions?

Evidence: Some auxiliary employees do not routinely use a district computer during their workday.

Action: Offer scheduled enrollment stations during shifts and an approved alternate factor.

Reinforcement - What will keep the change working after launch?

Evidence: Without routine follow-up, unenrolled new hires and dormant exceptions could recreate the original risk.

Action: Integrate MFA enrollment into onboarding, review exceptions monthly, and include MFA status in identity-security reporting.
