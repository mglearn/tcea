# K-12 Technology KPI Catalog
Use this catalog as a starting point, not as a requirement to track everything. A good executive dashboard normally contains a small number of measures that answer current leadership questions.

> **GenAI data guardrail:** Use fictional, public, aggregated, or properly sanitized information with public generative AI tools. Do not enter student records, staff records, personally identifiable information, credentials, sensitive cybersecurity details, legal records, confidential procurement material, vendor-confidential information, or sensitive district project information unless your district has explicitly approved the tool and data use.
## Project Management

| KPI | What It Measures | Example Formula | Direction | Cadence | Typical Audience |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Milestones completed on time | % of due milestones completed by planned date | On-time due milestones / all due milestones x 100 | Higher | Biweekly | Project sponsor / cabinet |
| Schedule variance | Difference between planned and actual milestone timing | Actual days - planned days | Toward 0 | Biweekly | Project lead |
| Budget variance | Difference between approved/forecast spending and actual/committed spending | Actual or committed - planned | Toward 0 | Monthly | CTO / CFO |
| Open high risks | Unresolved risks rated high for probability and impact | Count | Lower | Weekly | Project sponsor |
| Scope changes approved | Approved additions/removals that change baseline scope | Count and estimated impact | Context | Monthly | Project sponsor |
| Decisions overdue | Leadership decisions past the date needed to protect schedule | Count | Lower | Weekly | Cabinet / sponsor |
| Benefits realized | % of defined benefits meeting agreed evidence criteria | Benefits achieved / benefits due x 100 | Higher | Quarterly | Sponsor / cabinet |
| Stakeholder confidence pulse | Favorable response to a short project confidence question | Favorable responses / all responses x 100 | Higher | Monthly | Project lead |

## How to Use the Catalog

1. Start with the decision or leadership question.
2. Select the smallest set of KPIs that answers it.
3. Define the formula and data source locally.
4. Establish a baseline before setting an ambitious target.
5. Name an owner for the measure and an owner for the response.
6. Decide what action is triggered when the KPI is off target.
7. Review whether the KPI is still useful after the project or service stabilizes.
