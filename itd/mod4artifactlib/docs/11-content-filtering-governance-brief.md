# Content Filtering Governance Brief

**Ardent ISD | Leadership Framework**

## Purpose

Content filtering is both a technical service and a governance responsibility. The technology department operates the filtering platform, but decisions about instructional access, policy, legal requirements, student safety, and exceptions should not rest with one technician or one department acting alone.

## Decision Roles

| Decision Area | Primary Role | Partners / Escalation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Technical operation and reliability | Technology Services | Network/security specialists; vendor support |
| Category configuration and standard rules | Technology leadership | Curriculum, student services, administration |
| Instructional exceptions | Campus/curriculum process | Technology for implementation; administration for disputed requests |
| Policy alignment | District administration | Board policy resources and legal counsel as needed |
| CIPA-related compliance review | District leadership | E-Rate lead/consultant, legal/policy resources, Technology |
| Security-driven blocking | Technology/security | CTO, cybersecurity specialists, executive leadership as appropriate |
| Appeals / contested access | Defined district process | Campus leadership, curriculum, student services, legal/policy resources as needed |

## Leadership Principles
- Filtering decisions should be documented and reviewable.
- Technical staff should not be required to resolve policy or legal disputes independently.
- Overblocking can interfere with teaching and learning; underblocking can create safety, policy, and compliance concerns.
- Changes should be evaluated for impact on students, staff, testing, accessibility, and legitimate instructional use.
- The district should maintain a clear exception and escalation process.

## Annual Review Questions
1. Is the district's internet safety policy current and aligned with its filtering practices?
2. Are filtering categories and exception processes documented?
3. Are instructional complaints concentrated in particular categories or services?
4. Does the district know who may approve exceptions and how quickly?
5. Are filtering changes tested for unintended effects?
6. Are required E-Rate/CIPA documentation responsibilities assigned to the correct district roles?

## CTO Communication Line

When discussing filtering with cabinet or the board, explain **the purpose, decision process, evidence, and tradeoffs**. Avoid reducing the conversation to a list of blocked categories.
