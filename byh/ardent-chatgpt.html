Most Asked Questions

Short answers are navigation aids. Open the linked full section before acting.

What should I do if I will be absent, late, or need to leave early?

Complete the district Absence Request form, speak with the principal, and obtain the principal’s approval. Call Ms. Reyna Caldwell first, then immediately call Ms. Trina Holloway so a substitute can be requested.

Official wording
“Only the principal can approve an absence or give permission to leave campus.”

Source: Handbook, pages 2-3.

What are the staff work hours?

Staff are expected to sign in and be at their assigned places by 7:30 a.m.; Educational Associates by 7:15 a.m. The typical workday is 7:25 a.m. to 4:00 p.m.; Educational Associates work 7:15 a.m. to 3:45 p.m. Staff may need to remain for required meetings and events.

Source: Handbook, page 4.

Open the full related section

When may staff use personal cell phones or devices?

Barring extenuating circumstances, staff should not use cell phones or other electronic devices during the workday. Staff should also keep personal devices stored during instruction to model the scholar device expectation.

Source: Handbook, pages 3-7.

Open the full related section

May staff use generative AI tools?

Staff may use district-approved generative AI tools for planning, communication, and administrative tasks. Do not enter confidential scholar data, personally identifiable information, or protected records into an unapproved tool. Review AI-generated material for accuracy and age appropriateness before using it with scholars.

Official wording
“When in doubt about whether a tool is approved, contact the technology department before use.”

Source: Handbook, page 3.

Open the full related section

When must suspected child abuse, neglect, or trafficking be reported?

The employee must make the report no later than 48 hours after first suspecting abuse or neglect. The duty cannot be delegated, and the employee may not rely on the campus to report on the employee’s behalf.

Official wording
“A report must be made no later than 48 hours after the employee first suspects abuse or neglect.”

Source: Handbook, page 7.

Open the full related section

How do I report a work-related accident or injury?

Report the injury immediately to your supervisor, then to the Business Manager. Also inform Ms. Caldwell or a leadership-team member. Accidents must be reported within 24 hours.

Source: Handbook, page 3.

Open the full related section

What should I do when a scholar becomes ill?

Send the scholar to the clinic with a clinic pass. The nurse is the only person at school who can send a scholar home for illness. Teachers and scholars may not call a parent for pickup without the nurse’s permission.

Source: Handbook, page 8.

Open the full related section

What are the rules for classroom celebrations and birthday treats?

Without prior principal approval, three classroom parties per year are allowed. Birthday treats must comply with nutrition rules, be store-bought, and be served during the last few minutes of the school day. Special deliveries remain in the office until the end of the instructional day.

Source: Handbook, page 8.

Open the full related section

What approvals and deadlines apply to a field trip?

The principal must approve every off-campus field trip. Submit the request form at least one month in advance. Scholars need signed permission, and adults must complete the volunteer application and pass the required background check.

Source: Handbook, page 12.

Open the full related section

How many grades are required each six weeks?

Teachers must collect at least two daily formative grades per week, totaling 12 per six-week period, and three major summative grades per six-week period. Daily grades are 35% and major grades are 65% of the six-weeks grade.

Source: Handbook, pages 12-13.

Open the full related section

What happens after a scholar receives a failing grade?

Within 10 days, the scholar receives intervention and an opportunity to redo the work or complete an alternate assignment. The original and redo grades are averaged. Teachers have five days from entry of the failing grade to submit the Assignment Redo Request Form.

Source: Handbook, page 13.

Open the full related section

When are weekly lesson plans due?

Weekly lesson plans for the following week are due in the teacher folder on Fridays by 3:45 p.m.

Source: Handbook, page 15.

Open the full related section

When are emergency lesson plans due?

Every teacher must file three days of emergency lesson plans with the office clerk by the Friday before Labor Day.

Source: Handbook, page 11.

Open the full related section

What is the staff dress expectation?

Needs review

Staff must maintain professional dress and behavior. Jeans are allowed only on Friday or on other days specified by administrators. The handbook does not provide a more detailed campus dress standard.

Source: Handbook, page 18.

Open the full related section

How do I request supplies or reimbursement?

Submit supply requests to the campus secretary at least one week before the materials are needed. Obtain prior approval before buying materials with personal funds. Reimbursement is limited to $100, requires a receipt, and does not include sales tax.

Source: Handbook, page 20.

Open the full related section

What should I do if I believe I have been harassed?

Confer with a school administrator immediately. The handbook states that sexual harassment will not be tolerated and refers employees to district Policy DHC and DIA.

Source: Handbook, pages 19-20.

Open the full related section

When are progress reports and parent contacts required?

Progress reports are sent at the end of the third week of each reporting period. A conference is required after the first report card and each six weeks for failing scholars or scholars in danger of retention. Required parent contacts should be documented and made within 24 hours.

Source: Handbook, page 17.

Open the full related section

What are the basic evacuation expectations?

Leave immediately when the evacuation signal is given, close the classroom door, follow the posted route, take the emergency bag and roll sheet, check attendance in the safe zone, and wait for the all-clear before returning.

Source: Handbook, page 9.

Report an absence, lateness, or early departure

  1. Complete the district Absence Request form.
  2. Speak with the principal and obtain approval.
  3. Call Ms. Reyna Caldwell first at (210) 555-4821.
  4. Immediately call Ms. Trina Holloway at (210) 555-6390 so she can request a substitute.
  5. If leaving campus, sign out in the front office.

Report an employee accident or injury

  1. Notify your supervisor immediately.
  2. Notify the Business Manager.
  3. Inform Ms. Caldwell or a campus leadership-team member.
  4. Complete required reporting within 24 hours.

Source: Handbook, page 3.

Send an ill scholar to the clinic

  1. Complete a clinic pass and note the time the scholar leaves class.
  2. Send the scholar to the nurse.
  3. Do not call a parent for illness pickup without the nurse’s permission.
  4. Follow medical restrictions communicated by the nurse.
  5. Send any medical note or medicine directly to the nurse.

Report suspected child abuse, neglect, or trafficking

  1. Act when you first suspect abuse, neglect, or trafficking.
  2. Make the report to the proper authorities yourself.
  3. Use the 24-hour Child Abuse Hotline, 1-800-252-5400, or the online reporting option named in the handbook.
  4. Complete the report no later than 48 hours after first suspicion.
  5. Consult the counselor only when you need help understanding what may constitute abuse, neglect, or trafficking; do not delegate the report.

  2. Report the incident to the principal immediately.

  4. Cooperate with the administration’s investigation and intervention.

Evacuate during a drill or emergency

  1. Leave the room immediately when the evacuation signal is given.
  2. Close the classroom door.
  3. Take the emergency bag and roll sheet.
  4. Follow the posted evacuation route.
  5. Move to the safe zone, face the building, and check the class roll.
  6. Keep scholars in line using a quick walk and no talk.
  7. Wait for the all-clear before returning.

Source: Handbook, page 9.

Use the campus discipline process

  1. Hold a private one-on-one restorative conference with the scholar.
  2. If behavior does not change, restate the problem and desired change, then assign a consequence.
  3. Contact the parent for a phone or personal conference.
  4. Refer the scholar to the office.
  5. When referring, call, email, and send a note so adults receive clear information.

Important: Refer immediately for serious situations listed in the handbook, including physical altercations, severe threats, dangerous objects, drugs, alcohol, tobacco, or weapons.

Source: Handbook, page 10.

Bullying Prevention and Intervention pages 5-6

POLICY FFI / TEXAS BULLYING LAW

Bullying occurs when a student or group of students engages in written or verbal expression, expression through electronic means, or physical conduct that occurs on or is delivered to school property, at a school-sponsored or school-related activity, or in a vehicle operated by the District, and that:

  1. Has the effect or will have the effect of physically harming a student, damaging a student's property, or placing a student in reasonable fear of harm to the student's person or of damage to the student's property; or
  2. Is sufficiently severe, persistent, and pervasive enough that the action or threat creates an intimidating, threatening, or abusive educational environment for a student.

This conduct is considered bullying if it exploits an imbalance of power between the student perpetrator and the student victim through written or verbal expression or physical conduct, and interferes with a student's education or substantially disrupts the operation of a school.

Bullying is prohibited by the district and could include hazing, threats, taunting, teasing, confinement, assault, demands for money, destruction of property, theft of valued possessions, name-calling, rumor spreading, or ostracism. In some cases, bullying can occur through electronic methods, called "cyberbullying," including bullying that occurs off school property or outside a school-sponsored event if it interferes with a student's educational opportunities or substantially disrupts the orderly operation of the campus.

If a scholar believes he or she has experienced bullying or has witnessed bullying of another scholar, it is important for the scholar or parent to notify a teacher, counselor, principal, or another district employee as soon as possible to obtain assistance and intervention. The administration will investigate all reported allegations of bullying. Teachers who witness or believe they have witnessed bullying must report the incident to the principal immediately.

UPDATED Consistent with David's Law and current Texas Education Code requirements, the district will notify the parent of a scholar alleged to be a victim within three business days and notify the parent of the scholar alleged to have engaged in the bullying within a reasonable time. Anonymous reporting is available. Any retaliation against a scholar who reports bullying is prohibited.

If the results of an investigation indicate that bullying has occurred, the administration will take appropriate disciplinary action when the behavior falls within the established authority of the school district. The administration will inform the parent if the behavior is outside the authority and jurisdiction of the district and advise on any known options for addressing the behavior. The district will contact the parents of the victim and of the scholar who was found to have engaged in bullying. Available counseling options will be provided to these individuals, as well as to any scholars identified as witnesses. The principal may, in response to an identified case of bullying, decide to transfer a scholar found to have engaged in bullying to another classroom at the campus. The board has established policies and procedures to prohibit bullying and to respond to reports of bullying with investigation and intervention as necessary.

Lesson Plans page 15

Lesson plans are to be turned in weekly on Fridays by 3:45 p.m. in your teacher folder for the following week. Plans should include, but are not limited to, the lesson frame ("I will, we will" statements), the gradual-release model of the lesson cycle, recognize-and-reinforce opportunities, formal and informal assessment, and research-based best practices.

There are two kinds of lesson plans for the regular teacher, plus a third kind for substitute teachers.

1. Long-range plans

  • Are for a week, month, semester, or year.
  • Provide a tentative itinerary of what and when.
  • Must be continually monitored and adjusted.
  • Include distributed practice.

2. Daily plans

  • Determine, based on today's lesson, what will be taught tomorrow.
  • Should represent the next step beyond what the scholars already know.
  • Involve decisions about what to include and exclude for a clear presentation that speeds learning.

The daily planning process consists of mental steps that, depending upon teacher expertise and style, may or may not need to be recorded. The extent of written detail should be determined by the teacher and principal.

  1. Diagnose. Ascertain the next new learning needed by a scholar or group of scholars.
  2. Task-analyze. Break a broad generalization or concept into subsets or sub-skills, then into long-term objectives and daily objectives.
  3. Set the daily objective. Confirm there is a good purpose, that the scholar can transfer the concept or skill, and that the objective is small enough to teach in a 20- to 30-minute lesson. Write the objective so observable validation can be made that learning has taken place.
  4. Check independent practice. Confirm the practice requires the new learning, that scholars have the necessary skills, and that it requires thinking rather than guessing.
  5. Plan practice. For a 20- to 30-minute period of independent work, plan mass practice of the new objective with varied activities and practice of different objectives.
  6. Distribute practice. After the information is initially learned, distribute practice over a long period to increase retention.

Response to Intervention (RTI) pages 18-19

RTI is a tiered framework for service delivery that is systematic and data-based for identifying and resolving scholars' academic or behavioral difficulties through implementation of scientifically based instructional practices based on the individual learner's needs. This framework provides high-quality instruction and intervention matched to the scholar's needs, frequent assessment through progress monitoring to make decisions about changes in instructional strategies

  • and goals, and child-response data applied to educational decisions, including special-education eligibility determination for scholars suspected of having learning disabilities.
  • Key Components of RTI
  • The use of teaching methods and interventions proven to work because they are research-based.
  • The tracking of progress of scholars receiving the interventions.

The use of data derived from monitoring scholar progress to make important educational decisions, including providing more intensive interventions when progress is not adequate and considering special-education resources when a scholar does not respond to interventions of increasing intensity.

RTI meetings will be held every three weeks. RTI protocols can be found on the district's webpage.
