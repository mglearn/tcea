Most Asked Questions

Who do I contact when I am going to be absent, late, or leaving early?

Complete the district's Absence Request form and speak with the principal. Only the principal can approve an absence or give permission to leave campus. Call Ms. Reyna Caldwell first, then contact Ms. Trina Holloway, who requests the substitute.

Official wording "Contact Ms. Reyna Caldwell first at (210) 555-4821 by phone. After informing Ms. Caldwell, immediately contact Ms. Trina Holloway at (210) 555-6390. Ms. Holloway will request the substitute."

How many absences are considered excessive?

The District considers all absences beyond 10 days in a school year as excessive. The campus principal must notify Human Resources if this happens. Unapproved leave results in the teacher being docked.

Source: Handbook, pages 2–3

Can scholars use cell phones or personal devices during the day?

No. Under House Bill 1481, scholars may not use a personal wireless device (cell phones, smartwatches, personal tablets) during the instructional day. Devices must be silenced and stored out of view. Exceptions apply for a device required by an IEP, Section 504 plan, or individual health plan, or as directed by a licensed provider. NEW 2026–2027

What are the staff arrival and departure times?

Staff sign in on entry and are at their places by 7:30 a.m. (7:15 for Educational Associates). The typical workday is 7:25 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. (7:15 to 3:45 for Educational Associates). Staff work until the day's work is complete, including meetings and events. Leaving before 4:00 p.m. requires principal approval and signing out.

What should I do if I suspect child abuse or neglect?

Report suspected abuse, neglect, or trafficking no later than 48 hours after you first suspect it. Call the 24-hour Child Abuse Hotline at 1-800-252-5400 or report online. You cannot delegate this duty. Failure to report is a crime. If unsure what counts, consult the counselor. UPDATED

Official wording "A report must be made no later than 48 hours after the employee first suspects abuse or neglect. … An employee may not delegate this duty to another person and may not rely on the campus to make the report on the employee's behalf."

What do I do if a scholar becomes ill or is injured?

Give the scholar a clinic pass with the time they leave. The school nurse is the only person who can send a scholar home for illness, and only the nurse gives permission to call parents for illness. For serious injury, notify the principal and nurse. In a life-threatening emergency with no time to consult, the responsible employee uses judgment on emergency assistance and still notifies the nurse and administrator.

Source: Handbook, page 8

How do I report bullying that I witness?

Teachers who witness or believe they witnessed bullying must report it to the principal immediately. The administration investigates all reported allegations. Parents of an alleged victim are notified within three business days. Retaliation against a scholar who reports is prohibited.

Source: Handbook, pages 5–6

What approvals do I need for a field trip?

All off-campus field trips need principal approval in advance. Submit the field-trip request form at least one month in advance. No scholar leaves campus without a signed permission slip. Trips depart no earlier than 8:45 a.m. and return no later than 2:00 p.m. Adults who attend must complete the online volunteer application and pass the background check.

How many grades must I record each six weeks?

Collect at least two daily (formative) grades per week, totaling 12 per six-week period (35% weight), and three major (summative) grades per six-week period (65% weight).

Source: Handbook, pages 12–13

What happens when a scholar receives a failing grade?

Within 10 days of a failing grade, the scholar receives intervention and a chance to redo or complete an alternate assignment. The new grade is the average of the original and the redo. Teachers have five days from entering the failing grade to submit the Assignment Redo Request Form. Parents must be notified of failing grades, and a conference is required.

Source: Handbook, page 13

Step-by-Step Procedures

Reporting an absence, late arrival, or early departure

  1. Complete the district's Absence Request form.
  2. Speak with the principal. Only the principal can approve an absence or permission to leave campus.
  3. Call Ms. Reyna Caldwell first at (210) 555-4821.
  4. After informing Ms. Caldwell, contact Ms. Trina Holloway at (210) 555-6390.
  5. Ms. Holloway requests the substitute.
  6. If you will be absent more than three days, personally complete the necessary form and confer with the principal and senior administrative assistant.
Source: Handbook, pages 2–3

Reporting suspected child abuse, neglect, or trafficking

  1. Make the report yourself — you cannot delegate it.
  2. Call the 24-hour Child Abuse Hotline at 1-800-252-5400, or report online at the Texas Abuse Hotline website.
  3. Report no later than 48 hours after you first suspect abuse or neglect.
  4. If unsure what constitutes abuse, neglect, or trafficking, consult the counselor.
Source: Handbook, page 7

Reporting a work-related accident or injury

  1. Report the accident within 24 hours.
  2. Inform Ms. Caldwell or a member of the leadership team.
  3. Report to your supervisor first, then to the Business Manager.
  4. If unable to work from the injury, you will be notified of rights under the Texas Labor Code.
Source: Handbook, page 3

Requesting a field trip

  1. Confirm the trip is educational and tied to the curriculum.
  2. Get the field-trip request form from the office.
  3. Submit it to the principal for approval at least one month in advance.
  4. The Director of Transportation determines bus availability.
  5. Send an itinerary and permission form home; no scholar leaves without a signed permission slip.
  6. Attending adults complete the online volunteer application and pass the background check.
Source: Handbook, page 12

Requesting supplies or reimbursement

  1. Submit a supply request to the campus secretary at least one week before the date needed.
  2. For personal purchases, get prior approval before buying.
  3. Keep the receipt (sales tax is not reimbursed).
  4. The district reimburses up to $100.
Source: Handbook, page 20

Handling a failing grade and redo

  1. Within 10 days of the failing grade, provide intervention and offer a redo or alternate assignment.
  2. Submit the Assignment Redo Request Form within five days of entering the failing grade.
  3. Average the original grade and the redo grade for the new grade.
  4. Notify parents of the failing grade and document contact.
  5. Schedule a conference with the parent or guardian.
Source: Handbook, page 13

Responding to an evacuation or lockdown

  1. On the evacuation signal, leave the room immediately and close the classroom door.
  2. Follow the route on the evacuation diagram.
  3. Take the emergency bag and roll sheet.
  4. Move to the safe zone, turn to face the building, and keep scholars in line (quick walk, no talk).
  5. Wait for the all-clear signal before returning.
  6. For a lockdown, follow the Standard Response Protocol; anyone may institute a lockdown if a dangerous situation will ensue.
Source: Handbook, page 9

Preparing a substitute folder

  1. Create a clearly labeled folder and turn it in to the office by the end of the third week of school.
  2. Include the daily instructional schedule and special pullout times (library, computer lab).
  3. Include schedules for special scholars (resource, speech), and modifications for special needs.
  4. Include lunch and duty schedules, discipline plan and forms, and a calendar of special activities.
  5. Include supplementary activities and pertinent scholar information such as food allergies.
  6. Update the plans periodically.
Source: Handbook, page 20

Filing emergency lesson plans

  1. Prepare three days of emergency lesson plans.
  2. File them with the office clerk by the Friday before Labor Day.
  3. Include the daily schedule, duty assignments, morning routine, bell schedule, building map, seating chart, dismissal plan, early-finisher activities, emergency exit procedures, and two reliable scholars plus a nearby staff contact.
Source: Handbook, page 11

Introduction and District Information

Introduction page 2

This handbook provides information to help staff succeed. Not all district policies are included; those that are have been summarized. It is neither a contract nor a substitute for the official district policy manual, and it does not alter the at-will status of non-contract employees. Policies can change at any time, and those changes supersede any conflicting handbook provision. District policies are online at www.ardentisd.net. This campus handbook supplements, and does not replace, the Ardent ISD Employee Handbook.

Mission statement (Policy AE) page 2

The mission of Ardent ISD is to provide an educational environment that enables all students to develop essential academic skills for a lifetime of learning and to prepare them to be responsible, contributing citizens in a diverse and changing world. The motto is "Attitude is a Choice."

New for 2026–2027 NEW page 2

New or updated items reflect recent Texas legislation and Board policy: statewide student cell-phone restrictions (H.B. 1481), a display and a daily prayer or reflection period (S.B. 10 and S.B. 11), armed-intruder response and mental-health training requirements, uniform grade-reporting and parental-notification expectations, artificial-intelligence acceptable use, and updated child-abuse and human-trafficking reporting duties. Where any handbook provision conflicts with state law or Board policy, state law and Board policy control.

Attendance and Work Hours

Employee attendance — absences, arriving late, leaving early pages 2–3

Every employee provides the best possible education for every scholar every day. Complete the Absence Request form and follow procedures. Personal (discretionary) leave may not be approved during critical events such as the first day of school, days before and during STAAR testing, the day before or after a holiday, the last day of school, and special school-wide events. For absences of more than three days, personally complete the necessary form and confer with the principal and senior administrative assistant. Absences beyond 10 days per year are excessive and reported to Human Resources. Unapproved leave is docked.

Official wording "Contact Ms. Reyna Caldwell first at (210) 555-4821 by phone. After informing Ms. Caldwell, immediately contact Ms. Trina Holloway at (210) 555-6390. Ms. Holloway will request the substitute."

Arrival and departure (staff) page 4

Staff sign in on entry and are at their places by 7:30 a.m. (7:15 for Educational Associates). The typical workday is 7:25 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. (7:15 to 3:45 for Educational Associates). Staff work until the day's work is complete, including meetings and events. Leaving before 4:00 p.m. requires principal approval and signing out. Sign out in the front office when leaving during lunch or conference.

Attendance (scholar) page 4

The teacher takes attendance, monitored daily by the campus secretary. Take attendance at 8:45 a.m. If not taken by then, the attendance clerk notifies the teacher to take it immediately. A daily report documents the times attendance is or is not taken.

Faculty meetings and trainings page 12

Attendance at faculty meetings, team meetings, and planning is required. Sign-in sheets are distributed at every meeting. Failure to attend 90% of faculty meetings and trainings results in an unsatisfactory rating on the summative evaluation, with written notice before the summative conference.

Technology Use

Acceptable Use Policy — personal use of electronic media (Policy DH) page 3

Ardent ISD technology resources are used only for learning, teaching, and administrative purposes. Users sign the acceptable use policies yearly. Access is a privilege, not a right. Noncompliance may result in suspension of access or termination of privileges and other discipline. Knowingly bringing prohibited material into the school's electronic environment is subject to disciplinary action under district, state, and federal policy.

Artificial Intelligence (AI) acceptable use NEW page 3

Staff may use district-approved generative AI tools for planning, communication, and administrative tasks. Do not enter confidential scholar data or personally identifiable information into any tool that is not district-approved. Review AI-generated content used with scholars for accuracy and age-appropriateness. Scholar use follows the Scholar Code of Conduct. When unsure whether a tool is approved, contact the technology department first.

Scholar personal communication devices (Policy FNCE / H.B. 1481) NEW page 3

Under H.B. 1481, scholars may not use a personal wireless device (cell phones, smartwatches, personal tablets) during the instructional day. Devices are silenced and stored out of view in a designated location. Staff collect or direct storage at the start of the day and return them at dismissal. Exceptions apply for a device required by an IEP, Section 504 plan, or individual health plan, or as directed by a licensed provider. Report repeated violations to the office rather than confiscating devices for extended periods. Staff model the expectation by keeping personal devices stored during instruction.

Cell phones, emails, text messaging, phone calls (staff) page 7

Barring extenuating circumstances, staff should not be on cell phones or other electronic devices during the work day. Classroom phones stay at low volume. Check your mailbox twice a day. The Ardent ISD email system is for District use only. If electronic-media use interferes with job duties, the employee is subject to discipline, up to termination.

Technology procedures and expectations page 20

Classroom tablets and laptops are for instructional purposes, and may be used for administrative tasks. Do not load anything without proper licensure. Additional computers are in the library on the librarian's schedule. Teachers implement technology to enhance instruction and may request campus training.

Websites page 20

Class websites include the schedule, assignments and major projects, classroom rules, conference period, and other helpful information. Photos and postings follow District policy, and staff photos are dignified. Teachers update websites each week.

Student Safety and Health

Bullying prevention and intervention (Policy FFI) pages 5–6

Bullying is written or verbal expression, expression through electronic means, or physical conduct on or delivered to school property, at a school activity, or in a District vehicle, that harms or threatens a student or is severe, persistent, and pervasive enough to create an intimidating, threatening, or abusive environment. It includes cyberbullying, including off-campus conduct that disrupts the campus. Teachers who witness or believe they witnessed bullying must report to the principal immediately. The administration investigates all allegations. Parents of an alleged victim are notified within three business days; parents of the alleged aggressor within a reasonable time. Anonymous reporting is available and retaliation is prohibited.

Reporting child abuse of all types UPDATED page 7

Campus personnel must report suspected abuse, neglect, or trafficking no later than 48 hours after first suspicion. Call 1-800-252-5400 or report online. The duty cannot be delegated and cannot be left to the campus. Failure to report is a crime. Consult the counselor if unsure what constitutes abuse, neglect, or trafficking.

Nurse clinic procedures page 8

For illness, give a clinic pass with the time the scholar leaves. The nurse is the only person who can send a scholar home for illness, and only the nurse gives permission to call parents for illness. For sudden illness or serious injury, notify the principal and nurse. In a life-threatening emergency with no time to consult, the responsible employee uses judgment on emergency assistance and still notifies the nurse and administrator.

Health page 13

Non-serious injuries (cut, scrape, bloody nose) go to the clinic. For serious injury where movement may aggravate it, do not move the child; notify the office. An Accident Report Form is completed by the nurse when medical attention or parent pickup is needed. Scholars need a health slip except in an immediate emergency. Medicine requires medical forms on file; authorized medications are kept and given in the clinic per doctor's orders.

Medicine at school page 16

All medication is delivered to the nurse on arrival. Scholars may not carry any medication. A signed parent release must include the scholar's name, date, drug name, time, and dosage. Scholars with asthma or anaphylaxis may carry and use prescribed medication only with written authorization from a parent and provider on file, and after demonstrating proper use to the provider and nurse.

Crisis management plan (Policy CKC) page 9

Drills follow District policy; Ardent ISD uses the Standard Response Protocol. There is a minimum of one fire drill per month. The fire alarm sounds for fire drills; tornado and lockdown drills are announced over the intercom, though a lockdown may occur without warning. Anyone may institute a lockdown if a dangerous situation will ensue. On evacuation, leave immediately, close the door, follow the diagram, take the emergency bag and roll sheet, move to the safe zone, and wait for the all-clear.

Safety and security UPDATED page 19

Visitors report to the office and obtain a picture-ID sticker. All visitors, contractors, and volunteers are screened through the visitor-management system, including a sex-offender registry check, before a badge is issued. Exterior doors stay locked during the instructional day; staff must not prop open or bypass them and must challenge or report anyone without a visible badge. The Standard Response Protocol is posted in every classroom.

Defibrillator page 10

Every school has an accessible defibrillator on the wall by the cafeteria doors. The Medical Emergency Team and nurse are trained in its use.

Discipline and Behavior

Discipline philosophy and campus procedure page 10

Discipline is enforced consistently but flexibly. The plan follows PRIDE: persevere, relate, influence, determine, engage. Steps: hold a one-on-one restorative conference; if no change, restate the problem and assign a consequence; contact the parent; refer to the office. Parents should not first hear of difficulty from the office. Refer immediately to the office for physical altercations, threats of severe harm, dangerous objects, or possession of drugs, alcohol, tobacco, or weapons.

In-school suspension pages 10–11

ISS is social isolation used in severe or recurring cases. Teachers do not assign ISS. Scholars complete a social-emotional lesson. There is no academic penalty: the administrator places the scholar, grade-level material is provided, the homeroom teacher gathers assignments at the next break, and the teacher collects and grades the work afterward.

Classroom, playground, cafeteria, and lunch-supervision rules page 11

Post classroom rules and communicate them to parents; group punishment and humiliation are prohibited. Playground: monitor all areas, no throwing objects, no organized running on pavement, use equipment as designed, no games out of an adult's view. Cafeteria: walk to assigned tables, use Level 1 voices, raise hands to leave seats, keep areas clean. Lunch supervision includes an attention signal, dismissing one class at a time, and focused supervision.

Positive Behavior Interventions and Supports (PBIS) page 17

PBIS builds a positive school culture and shared responsibility. One scholar per homeroom is recognized each week during Dynamic Dillo Fridays. Expectation posters are placed in common areas.

Instruction, Grading, and Assessment

Grading requirements pages 12–13

Daily (formative) grades: at least two per week, 12 per six-week period, weighted 35%. Major (summative) grades: three per six-week period, weighted 65%. Unit assessments replace CBAs. Language Arts: grades 1–2 reading is 75% (writing/oral 25%, spelling 5%); grades 3–4 reading is 50% (writing/oral 50%, spelling 5%). Within 10 days of a failing grade, provide intervention and a redo or alternate assignment; the new grade is the average of the original and redo. Submit the Assignment Redo Request Form within five days of entering the failing grade. Notify parents and hold a conference for failing scholars. Make-up work: three days for each day of absence; corrections are done at school after re-teaching.

Lesson plans pages 11, 15

Lesson plans are due weekly on Fridays by 3:45 p.m. in the teacher folder for the following week. Plans include the lesson frame ("I will, we will"), gradual-release model, recognize-and-reinforce opportunities, and formal and informal assessment. Long-range plans cover a week to a year; daily plans move step by step from what scholars know. The planning process runs from diagnose, task-analyze, set the daily objective, check independent practice, plan practice, to distribute practice.

Emergency lesson plans page 11

File three days of emergency lesson plans with the office clerk by the Friday before Labor Day, including the daily schedule, duty assignments, morning routine, bell schedule, building map, seating chart, dismissal plan, early-finisher activities, exit procedures, and two reliable scholars plus a nearby staff contact.

Benchmark assessment (CBA/CFA) page 5

Periodic benchmarks in reading, writing, science, and math assess TEKS mastery and guide intervention. STAAR is given in the spring in reading, mathematics, and writing. Results are logged in the Scholar's Data Notebook.

Homework page 14

Homework is independent practice previously modeled by the teacher, with clear purpose, and something parents can help with. Regularly assigned Monday through Thursday, with STAAR practice for grades 3–4.

Progress reports, report cards, promotion/retention page 17

Progress reports go to all parents at the end of the third week of each reporting period. A parent-teacher conference is required after the first report card and each six weeks for failing scholars or those in danger of retention. Contact parents within 24 hours and document it; include behavioral information. Report cards are issued every six weeks. The campus must not exceed a 10% failure rate. Promotion and retention follow district policy.

Response to Intervention (RTI) pages 18–19

RTI is a tiered, data-based framework using research-based instruction, progress monitoring, and data for educational decisions, including special-education eligibility. RTI meetings are every three weeks. Protocols are on the district webpage.

Grade Placement Committee page 13

The GPC meets in April and May to decide each scholar's best interest, including summer school for promotion, retention, or placement. Members: administrator, counselor, intervention teacher, homeroom teacher, and parent.

Selecting appropriate enrichment materials page 19

Use "G"-rated materials; provide an alternate assignment if a parent does not grant permission. "PG-13," "R," and "X" materials are never used. Follow Board Policy EFE (Local) on copyright. Videos are cleared through the office and approved by the principal at least one week before showing; lesson plans reflect how enrichment materials teach the objectives and TEKS.

Communication

Announcements, pledges, and reflection (S.B. 11) NEW pages 3–4

Mornings begin with the Pledge of Allegiance, Texas Pledge, and a moment of reflection. Scholars and staff stand at attention; scholars who do not say the pledge sit or stand quietly. A designated, voluntary period of prayer or reading is offered for those with a signed consent form on file. Staff must not encourage, discourage, or comment on participation and supervise all scholars equally.

Correspondence page 9

Correspondence distributed to staff or scholars is provided to a leadership team member for review and approval. Weekly or monthly newsletters are expected. Daily notes to parents do not need approval unless sensitive; grade-level notes require principal review with a copy for the file.

Newsletters and webpage page 16

Grade levels create a monthly newsletter. Send written communication home in the Thursday folder. Topics include upcoming units, literacy and math tips, ESL vocabulary, calendars, upcoming assessments, and recognitions.

Parent Night page 16

Parent Night presents end-of-grade-level expectations, grading criteria, home practice, and the discipline-management system, plus a handout of the child's daily schedule.

News media page 16

The Communications Office handles all news-media inquiries, especially controversial ones. No administrator or employee contacts the media on their own to release negative or controversial information without the consent of Superintendent Dr. Marcus Vance or Assistant Superintendent Ms. Priscilla Marsh.

Mailboxes and master calendar pages 15, 6

Check faculty mailboxes at least three times daily; scholars are not sent to check mailboxes and are not allowed in the workroom. Formal written communication in mailboxes is first approved by an administrator. The single master calendar is kept by the school secretary; submit requests two weeks before the event.

Confidentiality — FERPA page 8

Keep scholar information confidential; only staff with a legitimate educational interest discuss individual scholar data. FERPA protects the privacy of educational records. Do not berate scholars or share personal opinions about them; see the counselor to discuss scholar concerns.

Sexual harassment (Policy DHC) page 19

Sexual harassment will not be tolerated. Laws protect employees and scholars from harassment. If you feel harassed, confer with a school administrator immediately. District policies are on the Ardent ISD website.

Travel, Purchasing, and Facilities

Field trips page 12

Off-campus trips need principal approval in advance and must be educational and curriculum-tied. Submit the request form at least one month ahead; the Director of Transportation determines bus availability. No scholar leaves without a signed permission slip. Trips depart no earlier than 8:45 a.m. and return no later than 2:00 p.m. Attending adults complete the online volunteer application and pass a background check; siblings may not ride the bus; no adult is left alone with uncleared scholars; no cursing, smoking, vaping, or alcohol.

Supplies and materials page 20

Submit supply requests to the campus secretary at least one week before needed. Get prior approval for personal purchases. The district reimburses up to $100; a receipt is required and sales tax is not reimbursed.

Collection of money, deposits, disbursement page 8

Money is not kept in the classroom; turn all money in to the office each day. Get principal permission before any money agreement with a vendor.

Building care and damage page 5

The building is everyone's responsibility. Guidelines include approved hallway displays, covering no more than 25% of hallway walls (fire code), sealed food containers, and no open flames. Report damage to Ms. Holloway, who completes a work order. Teachers keep classrooms properly ventilated and lighted and enter maintenance requests as work orders.

Copier, keys, custodians pages 9, 14

Each person is authorized to use the workroom copier; ask office staff for help. The secretary provides keys and proxy cards, which cannot be duplicated and must be returned at checkout; scholars may not use proxy cards, and lost keys or cards may be charged. Custodians handle cleaning; maintenance items go through a work order by Ms. Holloway.

School Life and Programs

Awards pages 4–5

Awards are based on achievement and attendance. Recognitions include Armadillo of the Year, Dynamic Dillo of the Week, Perfect and Outstanding Attendance, and Gold, Silver, and Bronze Honor Roll (grades 1–4). A Principal's Breakfast is held in May for A Honor Roll all year. Only listed awards are given at the ceremony; other classroom awards are the teacher's own. Scholars receive incentives and certificates each six weeks with the report card.

Cafeteria (Dillo Den) pages 6, 11

Breakfast is served 7:30–8:00 a.m. (PreK and K may differ). Follow the strict lunch schedule; give two weeks' notice to skip the cafeteria. Teachers escort scholars and do not leave them unattended. Scholars with excessive charges receive an alternate lunch. Eating in the classroom requires the teacher to stay, waiving the duty-free lunch.

Classroom celebrations page 8

Up to three classroom parties per year without prior approval. Foods of minimal nutritional value are not allowed in reimbursable-meal areas. Treats must be store-bought and served in the last few minutes of the day. Special deliveries (balloons, flowers, candy) stay in the office. Notify parents of non-curriculum celebrations and provide an alternative activity.

Child nutrition policy pages 7–8

State FMNV guidelines do not allow candy or non-juice drinks during the school day, and current guidance prohibits competing with the federal feeding program.

Campus committees and extracurricular activities pages 7, 11

Committees include a spirit committee, events, CATCH, Dillo Choir, beautification, incentives, Student Council, Safety Patrol, Dillo Daughters, Dillo Dudes, Horticulture, and Art. After-school activities include Chess, Robotics, Art, Choir, Student Council, and Coding. Staff are encouraged to sponsor a club.

Library and lounge page 15

Teachers remain with scholars during the library period on the librarian's schedule. The employee lounge is off-limits to children; keep it clean and do not buy sodas for scholars.

Playground / recess UPDATED page 16

Scholars in full-day programs receive daily unstructured recess with time for travel and restroom breaks. Teachers monitor with full visibility and circulate; recess is not a break time for staff. Follow campus playground rules and the master schedule.

Wellness program and VIPS pages 21, 20

A wellness program serves employees and scholars through the coach and CATCH committee. Volunteers (VIPS) are vital; all volunteers complete the online application and pass the required background check before working with scholars.

Committees, Planning, and Schedules

Campus Advisory Team (CAT) page 7

The CAT includes grade-level, special-education, and special-areas representatives, parents, community and business members, and a Central Office representative. It researches campus issues and is essential to the Campus Improvement Plan. Minutes are on file and open to the public.

Campus Improvement Plan (CIP) page 7

The CIP guides campus decision-making and is discussed quarterly during staff meetings. It is available at www.ardentisd.net.

Grade-level meetings and PLCs pages 13, 17

Grade-level teams meet weekly for coordination and uniformity; minutes go to the principal. PLCs meet weekly with the instructional leadership team to analyze data, review data notebooks, choose standards to monitor, and plan intervention. PLCs do not replace grade-level planning.

Daily schedules pages 9–10

Daily schedules help office staff locate teachers and scholars and let administrators schedule walkthroughs. Teams follow a consistent schedule, posted outside every classroom, with a copy to Ms. Holloway. Schedules are not changed without administration approval, since changes affect special-education teachers and specialists.

Science lab / classrooms page 19

Safety and security procedures, supervision, lab-safety contracts, orientation, and posted guidelines are essential. Grade-level leads report depleted items to the principal.

Staff Responsibilities and Coverage

Responsibilities of all teachers and staff page 18

Turn in lesson plans, stay current on research, follow all IEP modifications, keep classroom materials organized, plan as a team weekly, keep grades current (two per week per content), maintain records, be punctual, check email frequently, and participate in campus events. Maintain professional dress and behavior; jeans are only allowed on Friday or other days specified by administrators. Provide a caring, positive climate.

Responsibilities of grade-level chair / team leader page 18

Represent the grade level at leadership meetings, take and share notes, schedule weekly grade-level meetings, order and inventory materials, inform the office of special activities, act as textbook coordinator, set the team agenda, arrange field trips, and provide the librarian with audio/visual needs.

Substitute folder and split class lists page 20

Turn in a labeled substitute folder by the end of the third week with the daily schedule, special-scholar schedules, modifications, lunch and duty schedules, discipline plan and forms, a calendar of activities, supplementary activities, and pertinent scholar information such as food allergies. Keep a split class list near your desk, updated as the roster changes, with copies to the front office.

Accident reporting page 3

Report accidents within 24 hours to Ms. Caldwell or a leadership team member. Report work-related injuries to the supervisor first, then the Business Manager. Employees unable to work from a work-related injury are notified of rights under the Texas Labor Code.

Leaving school during the day (scholars) page 14

Scholars who leave for any reason sign out in the office and may not leave without written parent permission. Only in emergencies will the school phone parents for permission. Leaving without permission or signing out is an unexcused absence subject to discipline per the Scholar Code of Conduct.
