Top five barriers and risks

TCEA-001 · High

Shared navigation creates a noisy, confusing semantic outline

Page or workflow: Shared header on sampled tcea.org pages

User impact: Screen-reader users who navigate by heading may encounter a long, misleading outline before reaching the page. Decorative glyphs may be announced as meaningless characters.

WCAG: 1.3.1 Info and Relationships; 2.4.6 Headings and Labels; 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value

Next action: Use semantic navigation lists and links rather than heading elements for every menu item. Keep a logical page outline, add a main landmark and working skip link, and hide decorative icons with aria-hidden="true" or CSS-generated content. Give icon-only controls explicit accessible names.

TCEA-002 · High

No sitewide digital accessibility statement or barrier-reporting route was located

Page or workflow: Contact page and shared footer

User impact: A person who encounters a digital barrier may not know how to request an accessible alternative or how quickly the organization will respond.

WCAG: Governance control supporting conformance; no single WCAG success criterion

Next action: Publish a clearly linked accessibility page identifying WCAG 2.1 AA as the target, a monitored contact method, an alternate-format request process, response expectations, the last review date, and known limitations. Add “Report an accessibility barrier” to the footer and relevant transactional pages.

TCEA-003 · High

Convention accommodation instructions contain an outdated interpreter deadline

Page or workflow: Accessibility and Accommodations

User impact: Deaf and hard-of-hearing participants may receive incorrect instructions and could miss the actual accommodation-request window.

WCAG: 3.3.2 Labels or Instructions

Next action: Replace the stale deadline with the verified 2027 deadline or a current, non-expiring instruction. Add a content owner, automated annual review, and pre-event date check for all accommodation pages and registration forms.

TCEA-004 · High

Core user journeys depend on unverified third-party platforms

Page or workflow: Membership, event registration, community, chat, payment, and email links

User impact: A barrier in any vendor workflow can block a core transaction even when the public marketing page is usable.

WCAG: WCAG 2.1 Level A and AA across the complete process

Next action: Create a vendor inventory, obtain current ACRs tied to deployed versions, test representative complete processes, require remediation owners and dates, and add accessible alternatives for any blocked task.

TCEA-005 · Moderate

Prominent homepage graphics contain text but expose generic image names

Page or workflow: Homepage

User impact: Screen-reader users may miss the purpose and details of prominent promotions, and users who enlarge text cannot resize wording embedded in images.

WCAG: 1.1.1 Non-text Content; 1.4.5 Images of Text

Next action: Use real HTML text for promotional wording and calls to action. Where an image remains informative, provide concise purpose-based alt text. Use alt="" only when nearby text supplies the full equivalent.

Part Two

Executive Summary

Website reviewed: TCEA.org and selected TCEA-controlled or linked resources. Assessment mode: Standard review. Technical baseline: WCAG 2.1 Level A and Level AA.

TCEA’s public site provides extensive educational content, events, professional learning, membership information, contact details, policy pages, downloadable documents, and links to member and registration systems. TCEA identifies itself as a nonprofit member-based organization, so this report does not assign a federal Title II public-entity compliance date. The Title II technical standard remains relevant to public-school customers and to any TCEA service delivered on behalf of a state or local public entity.

The review found strengths in descriptive page titles, visible page headings on most pages, many meaningful image names, visible form labels, searchable PDF text, and a dedicated convention accommodations page. The largest risks are the shared navigation outline and decorative glyph exposure, the missing sitewide digital-accessibility support route, an outdated interpreter deadline, and critical dependence on untested third-party platforms.

Overall result: insufficient evidence for a definitive percentage. The provisional result is 34 percent based on 11 rated criteria, with low confidence. No critical barrier was confirmed, but the site carries high-risk indicators and requires live browser and assistive-technology testing.

TCEA-001 · High · Open

Shared navigation creates a noisy, confusing semantic outline

Page
Shared header on sampled tcea.org pages
URL
https://tcea.org/
Component
Mega-menu, breadcrumbs, and icon-font labels
Description
The retrieved document order repeatedly presents dozens of H4 menu headings and private-use icon characters before the page H1. Courses and resources breadcrumbs also expose isolated characters such as “E” and “5.”
User impact
Screen-reader users who navigate by heading may encounter a long, misleading outline before reaching the page. Decorative glyphs may be announced as meaningless characters.
Affected users
Blind and low-vision users, screen-reader users, voice-control users, and users with cognitive disabilities
Blocked or affected task
Navigate to the page’s main content and understand the site structure
WCAG criterion
1.3.1 Info and Relationships; 2.4.6 Headings and Labels; 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value
WCAG level
A / AA
Evidence source
Direct web retrieval of the shared page structure
Confidence
Moderate
Recommended correction
Use semantic navigation lists and links rather than heading elements for every menu item. Keep a logical page outline, add a main landmark and working skip link, and hide decorative icons with aria-hidden="true" or CSS-generated content. Give icon-only controls explicit accessible names.
Verification method
Inspect the DOM and accessibility tree; navigate headings and landmarks with NVDA and Firefox; complete a keyboard-only menu review.

Automated Findings

Part Ten

Long-Term Controls

  • Accessibility ownership: name an accountable owner and page-level content owners
  • Digital inventory: track websites, subdomains, apps, forms, documents, media, embeds, and vendor platforms
  • Procurement: require current ACRs, deployed-version evidence, warranties, remediation obligations, and retesting rights
  • Accessible templates: lock in headings, landmarks, form patterns, focus styles, error summaries, and image guidance
  • Staff training: train communications, event, marketing, course, document, and procurement staff
  • Prepublication checks: require image, heading, link, table, document, caption, and contrast checks before release
  • Recurring testing: combine automated scans, keyboard tests, screen readers, zoom/reflow, and user testing
  • Barrier reporting: provide a monitored route, confirmation, escalation, and alternate-format response
  • Remediation tracking: record severity, owner, due date, workaround, retest evidence, and closure
  • Release governance: retest shared templates and vendor workflows after updates
  • Documentation retention: retain scan results, manual test scripts, ACRs, contracts, remediation records, and user feedback
