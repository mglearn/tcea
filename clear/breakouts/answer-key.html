🍎 The Mixed-Up Lunchroom

Grades 3–5 · Logic & observation
LockAnswerReasoning
1 — Count3Pizza, tacos, salad = 3 hot lunches. Ana's sandwich came from home, so she isn't counted.
2 — Evidence (MC)AnaAna's note states a peanut allergy — direct evidence. No allergy clue exists for the others.
3 — WordAnaMenu says sandwiches came from home; only Ana has a sandwich.
4 — Order (sequence)🍕 Pizza → 🌮 Tacos → 🥗 SaladThe exact order the menu board lists the hot lunches. (The 🥪 sandwich pad is a distractor.)

Decoy clue: the "Lost dog poster" gives no tray information — a teachable example of irrelevant info.

📡 Signal Check: The Viral Claim

Grades 6–8 · Media literacy
LockAnswerReasoning
1 — Source (MC)The official school district websitePrimary, authoritative source — and it still lists summer break normally.
2 — Red-flag count4No author, no date, no source link, and "share before they delete" urgency = 4 red flags.
3 — WordverifyAccepts: verify, verifying, fact check, fact-check, factcheck. Verify before you amplify.
4 — Process order🛑 Pause → 🔎 Check → ⚖️ Decide → 📣 SharePause, check the source, decide, then share (or not). Reversing this spreads misinformation.
5 — Evidence sort (multi)Check: official district website + three local news outletsThose two are trustworthy primary/independent sources. Leave unchecked: the anonymous account, the "cousin's friend," and the like/share count — all noise.

Decoy clue: the unrelated weather alert models spotting a distraction.

🔬 Lab Report Lockdown

Grades 9–12 · Scientific reasoning
LockAnswerReasoning
1 — Sample size8Only 8 students — far too few to generalize.
2 — Control group (MC)A comparison (control) group that did NOT drink BrainFizzWithout a control group you can't isolate the drink's effect from the tutoring.
3 — Reasoning flaw (word)causationAccepts: causation, cause. "Correlation does not equal causation."
4 — Evidence strength (order)📣 Ad → 📄 Tiny self-funded study → 🧪 Independent replicated studyWeakest to strongest. Independent replication is the gold standard; an ad is weakest.

Decoy clue: the can-design memo separates marketing from evidence of efficacy.
